## Where the physics differences come from

Two separate mechanisms make a single mujoco_warp substep differ from a C-MuJoCo substep from the same state. (1) Contacts: at contact-rich lying states some contacts get a different pyramidal tangent frame (below; switching both sims to the elliptic cone removes most of it). (2) Force-saturated position actuators under `implicitfast`: mujoco_warp keeps the actuator damping term (-kv) in the implicit velocity derivative even when the actuator force is clamped by its force range, C MuJoCo does not (measured below: qDeriv diagonal, and a native run that keeps -kv for saturated actuators reproduces mujoco_warp's substep to ~1e-4 rad/s). The difference scales with how hard the saturated joints accelerate, so (2) dominates after pushes / impacts / large tracking errors, while in the quiet parts of the nominal get-up both mechanisms are small enough that the closed-loop numbers above agree.

Saturated actuators in the nominal native get-up (64 envs, evaluated at the start of each policy step), fraction of actuator-steps and the most often saturated actuators:

| phase | saturated fraction | most often saturated (fraction of steps) |
|---|---|---|
| 0-1 s | 7.1% | waist_pitch_joint 53%, right_ankle_roll_joint 52%, waist_roll_joint 38%, left_ankle_roll_joint 27%, left_wrist_pitch_joint 11%, left_ankle_pitch_joint 9% |
| 1-2.5 s | 5.0% | right_ankle_roll_joint 97%, left_ankle_roll_joint 48%, waist_pitch_joint 0% |
| 2.5-10 s (standing) | 6.9% | left_ankle_roll_joint 100%, right_ankle_roll_joint 100% |

Both ankle-roll actuators (force range 50 Nm) stay at their force limit for the whole standing phase, so any perturbation while standing immediately goes through saturated actuators (mechanism (2)).
